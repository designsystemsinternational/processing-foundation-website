Creating Out of Necessity: Exploring Music, Art, and Code

My name is JAIYN. I am a creative coder, musician, and creative technologist. As a Black queer person, I often find myself in boxes that are atypical, and that perspective has shaped the way my artistic practice emerged — out of necessity. Over time, I became attuned to customizing how I create.

This summer, I had the opportunity to carry that spirit into my work with the Processing Foundation as an Open-Source Software Intern, where I explored p5.sound by developing a MIDI sampler.

As an intern I contributed directly to p5.js repository, writing code, proposing ideas, and (after many, many drafts!) refining them with the help of mentors and maintainers. Everyday I got to circulate myself at the heart of all my disciplines. It felt like the genesis of understanding how my interests move as a unit.

One of my main contributions was creating and adding functionality to the colorContrast() feature. This is a new function in p5.js 2.0 that allows users to determine if the contrast between two colors meet Web Accessibility Standards. Working inside the p5.js repository, I went through frequent rounds of revision to ensure my contributions were clear, meaningful, and integrated effectively.

I was exposed to what it takes for accessible and equitable code to be produced and shared across multiple audiences. While my usual approach of illustrating a topic may feel fitting, it can sometimes contrast with what’s required to reach a broader audience. Or vice versa, crafting comprehensible solutions through an atypical approach can be refreshing, especially for people encountering software for the first time and coming from diverse backgrounds.

I cultivated a new perspective and tightened many screws across my existing disciplines. Diving into the ethics of technology and how it reaches people can feel like opening an endless Pandora’s box.

For me, accessibility helps smooth out roadblocks and shows that learning a new skill isn’t as hard as it might seem. The open-source internship echoed that same spirit with simplicity and shared knowledge. I had fun!

Exploring Sound With p5.js

Up until this point in my internship, most of my exploration had been centered around camera manipulation, particularly through the createCapture() function. Attending a workshop with Tommy Martinez, the pioneer of p5.sound.js, was an enriching experience. By the end of it, I had experimented with p5.sound.js more than ever before, and I’m certain this is where I’ll continue to exist, especially as a musician who works at the intersection of sound and technology.

It felt like the kind of workshop I’d take more than once. I still return to the materials and discover something new each time. I also want to dive deeper into Spatial p5 (a creative coding toolkit for rapid prototyping in immersive environments), which was a completely new concept to me when it was introduced earlier this summer. Overall, I’m excited to explore how I can bring all of these tools together to customize interactive performances that can be alchemized and felt by others who resonate with them.

Connecting My Creative Practices

Another highlight of the internship was bridging my musical, artistic, and technical interests in ways I hadn’t before.

This was my first time truly understanding how I show up to my practice, and my first time using music to make sense of technical concepts, usually it’s the other way around.

I love being able to show up in spaces as myself and have others resonate with something I once thought was scarce. There is, in fact, a whole collective of people creating out of necessity, whether because of limited resources, the need for representation, or the drive to build spaces that reflect who they are.

Through this experience, I also came to appreciate the spirit of open source more deeply. I knew the p5.js community was vast, but my knowledge was humbled when I saw just how much support and people are working behind the scenes. It feels like we are all derivatives of each other in certain ways and it’s beautiful to see so many shared interests, materialize in so many different ways.
