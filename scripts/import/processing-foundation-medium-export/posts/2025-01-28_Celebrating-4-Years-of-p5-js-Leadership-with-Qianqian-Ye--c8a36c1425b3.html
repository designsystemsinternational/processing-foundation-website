Celebrating 4 Years of p5.js Leadership with Qianqian Ye!

Interview with Qianqian Ye by Suhyun (Sonia) Choi

Qianqian Ye, our now former p5.js Project Lead, has played a remarkable role in shaping our creative open-source software project and community. Their 4 year tenure has been marked by incredible achievements, such as securing substantial funding and supporting a global community of contributors dedicated to making p5.js more inclusive and accessible. Through their leadership, the project has flourished with care and dedication. In this short interview, Qianqian speaks about their proudest accomplishments, the challenges they’ve faced, and the lessons they’ve learned along the way.

What are you most proud of accomplishing throughout your role as the p5.js Project Lead?

One of my proudest accomplishments as the p5.js Project Lead was successfully applying for and managing €450,000 in funding through two rounds of the Contribute Back to Open Source Challenge from the Sovereign Tech Fund. This funding was dedicated to enhancing the organization and accessibility of p5.js documentation. Over the course of 8 months, we collaborated with approximately 140 contributors around the world. Our team included experienced and first-time contributors engaged in various activities, including coding, designing, writing, translating, researching, and accessibility. This project not only made the p5.js documentation more inclusive and user-friendly, but also brought the p5.js community together.

Learn more about the Sovereign Tech Fund x p5.js Documentation Accessibility Project.

What moments were challenging, and how were you able to overcome them?

When I started my role a few years ago as the first full-time open-source project lead at the Processing Foundation, I had to get comfortable with uncertainty — figuring things out as I went, making mistakes, always learning, and feeling okay sharing my vulnerability with the community.

Women, queer people, and people of color are often less visible in the open-source software space. Can I cultivate a community that’s more accessible for people like me? Sometimes, I had doubts about whether or not I was good enough to be here, especially when fixing bugs, adding new features, answering issues, and merging pull requests. Releasing software turned out to be slower than I hoped. But I realized that creating an accessible community isn’t just about speed or perfection; it’s about showing up, learning, caring, and growing together. Building this space takes time.

“But I realized that creating an accessible community isn’t just about speed or perfection; it’s about showing up, learning, caring, and growing together. Building this space takes time.” — Qianqian Ye, former p5.js Project Lead

Engaging with p5.js contributors has brought me immense joy in this role. This includes celebrating new contributors when they make their first commit, recognizing the efforts of experienced contributors, observing how contributors support each other on GitHub issues, and adding contributors to the p5.js All Contributors list. Over the past three and a half years, we have added around 400 contributors to the p5.js All Contributors list, and we currently have about 700 contributors listed in the p5.js library GitHub repository today.

We are grateful for the incredible impact Qianqian has had on our open-source communities. Their dedication, hard work, honesty, and care have inspired us and left a lasting impression on the p5.js platform and its global community. As they reflect on their journey, it’s clear that their leadership extends beyond technical contributions; it’s about nurturing a community that allows all of us to contribute to and shape creative open-source tools.
