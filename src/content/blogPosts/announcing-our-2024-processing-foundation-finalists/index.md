This is our tenth year running the fellowship program and this year’s fellowship program is themed ‘Sustaining Community: Expansion & Access.’ We hope these mini-grants support our broader community outside our fellowship’s capacity.

Continuing with previous years, we asked applicants to address at least one of four Priority Areas, describing how their project responds to the concerns of the topic. The four priority areas were:

• Archival Practices: Code & New Media: Projects aimed at developing tools and platforms for archiving and preserving creative code/the digital, as well as new media archival practices.

• Open-Source Governance: Initiatives focused on creating governance models that promote equitable decision-making, inclusive communities, engagement, and sustainable growth in open-source projects.

• Disability Justice in Creative Tech: Tools and projects that advance access and promote disability justice within the realm of creative technology.

• Access & AI: Engaging AI technologies to create accessible solutions and improve inclusivity in digital spaces.

We received a record-breaking 346 applications this year and were able to award 10 finalists. Special thanks to our new Program Manager, Tsige Tafesse, who made this work possible! Thank you to all who applied to our fellowship. We hope to continue to be in community with you all and take steps to support your work in the future. This year was an especially rigorous review process due to the sheer volume of applications we received, so we would like to applaud everyone who applied for their efforts. We appreciate all your shared brilliance in our community and hope your work can be supported no matter what.

#### Mala Kumar (she/her) | Economic, Social and Cultural Rights in AI

![](FFtUMqnSqPPobK5F.webp)

*2024 Finalist Project: Economic, Social and Cultural Rights in AI*

Most big corporate tech “for good” efforts focus on compliance with laws that govern civil and political rights, such as not facilitating human trafficking or not violating copyright. Such efforts are increasingly being referred to as “Responsible Tech.” A ride-share company is much more likely to have a policy to compensate its gig economy drivers for passenger assault than to guarantee income levels. Since a significant portion of foundational AI technologies are controlled by fewer than 15 companies, a lack of attention to ESC rights and AI will affect everyone. If net labor is not replaced in already economically depressed areas and if governments do not provide universal social safety nets, large portions of the global population cannot afford to participate in the modern economy. Mala is working on a project to identify policies (corporate, organizational, and governmental), frameworks, and tools to better the effects of new and novel AI technologies on ESC human rights. Examples include proposed legislation to mitigate economic disparities from large-scale labor automation, policies to reduce health disparities from computer vision diagnostic tooling, or GenAI model and product threat assessment framework tooling to assess cultural autonomy.

#### Iz Paehr (they/none) | DISCO CRAWLER

![](b5f2W-yRfi60kf8P.webp)

*2024 Finalist Project: DISCO CRAWLER*

DISCO CRAWLER archives the wisdom that disabled people share on social media and turns crip knowledge into posters for web and print. DISCO stands for Disabled Community, crawling points to web technologies. The further availability of knowledge collected under hashtags such as #Ableismtellsme, #DisabilityTooWhite, #ActuallyAutistic, #AltTextPalestine, and more, is at the whim of proprietary platforms. DISCO CRAWLER wants to build an otherwise archive of holding, lovingly visualizing, and publishing the meaningful insights, critiques, and survival techniques that disabled people continue to share generously online amidst a global pandemic. The project centers on disabled communities and engages crawling as both a crip and web technology. Disabled people have famously crawled up the stairs of the U.S. American Capitol Building to demand equal rights. Crawling is also a technical term for when a program visits websites in search of certain information. DISCO CRAWLER is built on disabled histories of crawling and crawls on in search of crip wisdom.

#### Francisco Barretto (he/him) and Micaelle Lages (she/her) | Hacking the Algorithm: Underrepresented Communities Take Charge of AI

![](_osGF_1W1anmVsSu.webp)

*2024 Finalist Project: Hacking the Algorithm: Underrepresented Communities Take Charge of AI*

Hacking the Algorithm is a ml5.js educational initiative for underrepresented communities to decentralize the discussion of machine learning and social justice. Hacking the Algorithm aims to bridge the gap in access to creative technology by empowering underrepresented communities through the use of Machine Learning with ml5.js and p5.js. The project aims to develop an extensive workshop in Salvador, Brazil, focused on identifying possible social and artistic applications for these technologies and engaging participants to propose solutions to community challenges. The work involves developing a syllabus, executing a workshop, and developing open documentation that can serve as archives and a starting point for creatives interested in leading the same initiative elsewhere. The workshop will equip participants with technical skills and foster critical thinking about machine learning’s social and political implications, and how developers and artists can address these issues in their work through innovative use of AI. This project disrupts the centralized narrative around AI by equipping participants with the skills needed for AI creation in a region beyond the traditional tech hubs. Through this project we strive for a future where communities are not passive consumers but are encouraged to be active participants in AI, shaping a technology that addresses global challenges. This is an effort to amplify other voices and perspectives in the discussion about technology and AI.

#### Blair Subbaraman he/him | p5.fab

![](kPZsIApbQViAf9Lh.webp)

*2024 Finalist Project: p5.fab*

Artists and craftspeople have a deep knowledge of materials that are often not accounted for in contemporary digital fabrication tools. Software for machines like 3D printers is designed to translate a digital model to physical form as faithfully as possible. This not only forces practitioners to work against the software to explore creative possibilities but also makes it difficult to share work with others. To develop a community around creative digital fabrication workflows, p5.fab extends creative code from screens to machines to promote sketching with physical materials.

With p5.fab, practitioners can write creative code to control machines, interactively edit settings while the machine is running, and document the resulting material outcomes. The project explores how alternative interfaces for using machines can offer an entry point to code and computation that takes advantage of craft sensibilities. Ultimately, p5.fab seeks to cultivate a creative fabrication community that invites physical practitioners to engage and guide the development of digital fabrication software.

#### Phoenix Perry (they/she) | Embodied Archival Practices: Generating Real-Time Visuals using Small Data and Personal Archival Footage

![](01BlHy4cgJhnYz7r.webp)

*2024 Finalist Project: Embodied Archival Practices: Generating Real-Time Visuals using Small Data and Personal Archival Footage*

This research focuses on the interplay between Archival Practices: Code & New Media and Disability Justice in Creative Tech. Drawing from their background as a filmmaker, circuit bender, and live video artist, they aim to create new work that bridges their past analog practices with current creative coding techniques, centering their experiences of disability. At the heart of this project is a deeply personal archive: approximately 200 hours of DV, SVHS, and VHS footage documenting my live video performances and experiments from the late 1990s and early 2000s. This footage captures a pivotal moment in their artistic development, as they began modifying and hacking video mixers to create accessible tools that resonated with their body’s glitches and movements as their disability manifested. By engaging with this archive, they seek to preserve and reanimate this critical period of their creative history but also to explore new ways of generating real-time visuals that embody the lived experience of disability.

#### Ghislain Irakoze (He/Him) | AI Contextualization Interactive Toolkit

![](75OdOtoq9u6GtF9M.webp)

*2024 Finalist Project: AI Contextualization Interactive Toolkit. Credits: Wastezon.*

As AI deployment and adoption advance in Africa, interlinking it with existing social, economic, and cultural systems remains a paradoxical challenge that will deprive millions of Africans of their rights and belonging. ‘AI Contextualization Interactive Toolkit Project’ aims to examine the cultural and social adaptations in AI systems across East Africa to develop tailored frameworks that the AI global community can leverage in designing and deploying Africa-tailored AI solutions. It applies a focus group method research to engage at least 6 grassroots-level digital rights organizations to explore social and cultural necessities in AI systems contextualization. Leveraging the insights and recommendations from grassroots-led research, the project aims to develop an interactive toolkit comprising practical guides on social and cultural contextual considerations in designing and deploying localized AI systems. The toolkit will be presented to the global AI community, creating the needed locally informed resources to inform AI systems development.

#### Payton Croskey (she/her) | Preserving Liberatory Futures: An Afrofuturist Archive

![](dY9oDuGTM4yjWNi8.webp)

*2024 Finalist Project: Preserving Liberatory Futures: An Afrofuturist Archive*

Afrofuturism is a cultural movement that combines imaginings of the past, peculiarities of the present, and visions of the future. Creators in this domain challenge dominant narratives while embracing a “concept of seeing and creating the future from a perspective that has the lineage of an African seed, of something older and other than Western,” as described by writer and activist adrienne maree brown in Emergent Strategy. Although many dreamers work within this multidimensional space, their contributions are often unrecognized or undervalued in traditional computational environments. Additionally, questions surrounding who and what is to be archived are ever-present as politics over truth and facts are debated within schools and on social and media platforms.

#### Beccy Abraham (they/them) | Tiny Touch Instruments

![](LjEhNelMkd7YKtcT.webp)

*2024 Finalist Project: Tiny Touch Instruments*

Beccy has been creating a set of Tiny Touch Instruments (TTIs), touch-controlled musical instruments that run on a mobile web browser. The instruments have simple interfaces and are designed for intuitive interaction. TTIs give people easy access to communal music-making. They recontextualize smartphones, which often foster disengagement, into tools for connection and creativity.

To further this work, Beccy is interested in creating a set of accessible, beginner-friendly tutorials that teach people how to build TTIs. These tutorials would span web interactivity and graphics with p5.js, Web Audio with tone.js, and mobile development concepts. They would also ask users to consider the creative aspects of building a software instrument, including interaction design, sound design, and visualization. Ultimately, Beccy would like to develop an approach to in-person TTI workshops, where people with mixed backgrounds in coding and music could come together to build TTIs and jam with their newly created instruments.
